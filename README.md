## Introduction

Women’s Studies—often organized academically as Women’s, Gender, and Sexuality Studies—is an interdisciplinary field that examines how gender functions as a fundamental organizing principle of human society. Rather than examining women in isolation, the discipline investigates how gender norms, hierarchies, and power relations are constructed, maintained, and challenged across culture, law, the economy, and everyday life. Studying this field provides analytical tools to unpack assumptions about identity, recognize structural inequalities, and understand how social movements have reshaped political and civil rights over the past two centuries.

This curriculum is designed for independent learners starting with no prior formal background in sociology, critical theory, or gender studies. It prioritizes conceptual clarity, accessible language, and self-directed resources over academic jargon or classroom-dependent assignments. The focus throughout is on developing foundational literacy in the discipline's core ideas, historical turning points, and theoretical frameworks—not on technical research methodologies, qualitative coding, or writing an academic thesis.

### How to Navigate the Curriculum

The subjects are sequenced to help you build theoretical and historical understanding progressively:

* **Start with the foundations:** Begin with Introduction to Women's and Gender Studies to establish basic terminology, the distinction between biological sex and socially constructed gender, and core analytical frameworks.
* **Ground theory in historical context:** Follow with History of Feminist Movements and Feminist Theory. Understanding the waves of political mobilization clarifies why specific theoretical debates—such as the tensions between liberal egalitarianism, radical feminism, and socialist feminism—emerged when they did.
* **Integrate intersectional frameworks early:** Study Intersectionality and Black Feminist Thought before moving into institutional analysis. Grounding your study in this tradition ensures you understand how race, class, gender, and sexuality operate simultaneously as interlocking systems rather than isolated variables.
* **Apply frameworks to institutions and primary sources:** Once the theoretical base is established, you can study Sociology of Gender, Canonical Feminist Primary Texts, and Global and Transnational Feminisms in whatever order best fits your interests. Sociology demonstrates how gender operates across workplaces and families; the primary texts allow you to read landmark works in their original voice; and transnational feminisms de-centers Western perspectives by examining global labor, colonialism, and cross-border solidarity.

### Scope

This page contains only the core, foundational subjects that constitute a standard undergraduate curriculum in Women's Studies. It does not attempt to cover specialized subfields or advanced research seminars. After completing these core subjects, you can branch out into specialized areas such as queer theory, feminist science studies, and social reproduction theory in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Women's and Gender Studies

An interdisciplinary introduction to the field's fundamental concepts, examining the social construction of gender, systems of privilege and oppression, and foundational analytical vocabularies.

[Introduction to Women, Gender, Sexuality Studies (UMass Amherst Open Books)](https://openbooks.library.umass.edu/introwgss) - The primary open-access textbook for this subject, systematically introducing theoretical frameworks, binary systems, institutions, and social movements.

[Introduction to Women's & Gender Studies - First Edition (Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/1450) - An alternative or introductory companion to the UMass textbook, structured around modular thematic units with embedded multimedia resources.

[Introduction to Women's and Gender Studies (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/wgs-101-introduction-to-womens-and-gender-studies-spring-2023) - A structured university course framework designed to accompany the readings, providing detailed session outlines, reading lists, and analytical writing prompts.

[Gender and Sexuality: Applications in Society (University of British Columbia, edX)](https://www.edx.org/learn/gender-studies/university-of-british-columbia-gender-and-sexuality-applications-in-society) - An interactive, self-paced video course that serves as an accessible entry point for learners who prefer structured multimedia lectures alongside reading.

### History of Feminist Movements

The historical progression of feminist activism and reform from nineteenth-century suffrage and abolitionist organizing through second-wave liberation, third-wave coalition-building, and contemporary digital activism.

[Unit V: Historical and Contemporary Feminist Social Movements (UMass Amherst Open Books)](https://openbooks.library.umass.edu/introwgss/chapter/introduction-feminist-movements/) - A concise textual primer detailing the historical waves, pivotal legislative struggles, and internal tensions within nineteenth- and twentieth-century feminist activism.

[Women Have Always Worked (Columbia University / New-York Historical Society, edX)](https://www.edx.org/xseries/columbiax-women-have-always-worked) - A multi-part video lecture series led by historian Alice Kessler-Harris that examines women's labor, legal status, and social mobilization from the colonial era through modern civil rights.

[No Turning Back: The History of Feminism and the Future of Women (Estelle B. Freedman)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780345450531) - The canonical historical monograph for this subject, tracing how economic transformations, political rights, and cultural struggles generated global feminist movements.

### Feminist Theory

Major theoretical paradigms within feminist thought, including liberal, radical, Marxist and socialist, psychoanalytic, care-focused, postmodern, and queer frameworks.

[Feminist Thought: A More Comprehensive Introduction (Rosemarie Tong and Tina Fernandes Botts)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780367857936) - The core survey textbook for this subject, methodically breaking down each major feminist theoretical tradition, its central thinkers, and its primary internal debates.

[Feminist Thought (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/wgs-301j-feminist-thought-fall-2023) - A complementary undergraduate syllabus that provides weekly reading pathways and analytical keyword assignments to apply the theories covered in Tong and Botts.

[Feminist Theory: From Margin to Center (bell hooks, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/feministtheoryfr0002hook) - A foundational theoretical critique that challenges bourgeois white-feminist assumptions and demonstrates how theory must be grounded in the experiences of marginalized women.

### Intersectionality and Black Feminist Thought

The analytical framework for understanding how interlocking systems of oppression—including race, gender, class, and sexuality—operate simultaneously, developed through the Black feminist intellectual tradition.

[The Urgency of Intersectionality (Kimberlé Crenshaw, TED)](https://www.ted.com/talks/kimberle_crenshaw_the_urgency_of_intersectionality) - A concise introductory lecture by the legal scholar who coined the term, illustrating the consequences of using single-axis frameworks to understand social inequality.

[The Combahee River Collective Statement (BlackPast)](https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/combahee-river-collective-statement-1977) - The seminal 1977 primary manifesto that articulated the concept of interlocking oppressions and established identity politics as a tool for radical collective liberation.

[Sister Outsider: Essays and Speeches (Audre Lorde, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/sisteroutsideres0000lord) - A core essay collection addressing difference, language, silence, and the structural necessity of centering race and sexuality within feminist liberation.

[Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment (Patricia Hill Collins, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/blackfeministtho0000coll) - The definitive sociological monograph synthesizing the Black feminist epistemic tradition, outlining the matrix of domination, standpoint epistemology, and self-defined consciousness.

### Sociology of Gender

How gender functions as an institutionalized social structure shaping socialization, identity, interaction, power, and inequalities across work, the family, education, and the state.

[Sociology of Gender: An Equity Lens (Open Oregon Educational Resources)](https://openoregon.pressbooks.pub/socgender/) - An open textbook surveying how gender is socially constructed and maintained across primary social institutions like the labor market, healthcare, family, and media.

[Men, Boys and Masculinities (GRÓ GEST / University of Iceland, edX)](https://www.edx.org/learn/social-science/the-university-of-iceland-masculinities) - A complementary course focusing specifically on the social construction of masculinity, examining male power, socialization, and patriarchal expectations within the sociology of gender.

### Canonical Feminist Primary Texts

Influential historical and philosophical primary texts that shaped the intellectual lineage of feminism, read directly to understand key conceptual shifts in the field.

[A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (Mary Wollstonecraft, Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3420) - The foundational 1792 Enlightenment text arguing for equal rational capacity, civic virtue, and educational access for women.

[A Room of One's Own (Virginia Woolf, Project Gutenberg Australia)](https://gutenberg.net.au/ebooks02/0200791h.html) - Woolf's landmark 1929 essay analyzing the material and financial prerequisites required for women to participate in intellectual and artistic production.

[The Second Sex (Simone de Beauvoir)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780307277789) - The 1949 existentialist work establishing the foundational feminist premise that woman is defined as "the Other" in relation to man, famously asserting that one is not born, but becomes, a woman.

[The Feminine Mystique (Betty Friedan, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/femininemystique0000frie) - The 1963 second-wave text analyzing the structural isolation and malaise of mid-century suburban domesticity.

[Gender Trouble: Feminism and the Subversion of Identity (Judith Butler, Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/gendertroublefem0000butl) - The 1990 text that revolutionized feminist theory by introducing gender performativity and interrogating the naturalization of biological sex.

### Global and Transnational Feminisms

Feminist critique and activism centered outside Western frameworks, analyzing how gender intersects with imperialism, globalization, migration, and postcolonial state formation.

[Women Worldwide: Transnational Feminist Perspectives (Oregon State University Open Education)](https://open.oregonstate.education/womenworldwide/) - An open textbook covering global health, labor in the international economy, environmental politics, and grassroots women's organizing across the Global South.

[Feminism Without Borders: Decolonizing Theory, Practicing Solidarity (Chandra Talpade Mohanty)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780822330219) - The seminal theoretical text of transnational feminism, containing the landmark critique "Under Western Eyes," which cautions against Western feminist generalizations of "Third World women."

[Women and Global Activism in Art, Media and Politics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/wgs-109-women-and-global-activism-in-art-media-and-politics-fall-2023) - A university course syllabus that structures the study of transnational feminist mobilization, examining how cross-border movements confront state power, conflict, and neoliberalism.
